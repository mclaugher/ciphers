The Alphabet as Numbers

A = 0, B = 1, … Z = 25. The single most reusable tool in classical cryptography — keep it beside you.

Most pen-and-paper ciphers are really arithmetic on letters. The moment you number the alphabet 0–25, shifting, adding a keyword, or multiplying becomes ordinary maths done modulo 26 (i.e. wrap around after Z back to A).

Caesar shift by n
Encrypt: E(x) = (x + n) mod 26
Decrypt: D(x) = (x − n) mod 26
…where x is the letter's number above, and mod 26 means "wrap past Z back to A."

Worked example — shift of 3 (Caesar's own).

H → 7 + 3 = 10 → K
A → 0 + 3 = 3 → D
Y → 24 + 3 = 27 → 27 − 26 = 1 → B (wrapped)

The wrap on Y → B is the whole trick of modular arithmetic: 27 is past the end of the alphabet, so subtract 26 to come back around.
