Transposition Ciphers

The other family. Substitution changes which letters you write; transposition keeps the letters and changes where they sit.

Rail fence

zigzag down the rails, read row by row Write the message in a zigzag across n rails, then read each rail left to right, top rail first. Message WEAREDISCOVEREDFLEEATONCE, n = 3: W . . . E . . . C . . . R . . . L . . . T . . . E . E . R . D . S . O . E . E . F . E . A . O . C . . . A . . . I . . . V . . . D . . . E . . . N . . read rail 1, then 2, then 3 → WECRLTE · ERDSOEEFEAOC · AIVDEN ciphertext: WECRLTEERDSOEEFEAOCAIVDEN Few possible rail counts → brute-force by trying each, like Caesar.

Columnar transposition

grid under a keyword, read columns in key order
  1. Number the keyword's columns by alphabetical order of their letters (ties left→right).
  2. Write the message into the grid row by row; pad the last row with nulls (e.g. X).
  3. Read the columns top to bottom, in the numbered order, to get the ciphertext.
key: Z E B R A order: A=1 B=2 E=3 R=4 Z=5 5 3 2 4 1 M E E T M E A T T H E D O C K S X X X X read order 1,2,3,4,5 → MHKX ETOX EADX TTCX MEES

The crack

SymptomWhat it means
Ciphertext has normal English letter frequencies It's a transposition, not a substitution — letters were moved, not swapped.
Letters look scrambled but all "belong" Break by anagramming: try column counts / rail counts and rearrange into sense, using common digraphs (TH, HE) and words to guide.

Key fact: if the plaintext had fourteen E's, so does the ciphertext. Transposition cannot hide letter frequencies — only letter positions.

Why it matters

Alone, transposition is weak. Combined with substitution it is not: Shannon called the two jobs diffusion (transposition — spread the structure out) and confusion (substitution — hide the relationship to the key). Modern block ciphers like AES alternate both for many rounds — a substitution–permutation network.
