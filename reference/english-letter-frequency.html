English Letter Frequency

The fingerprint of the language. A substitution cipher hides which letter you wrote — but never how often you wrote it. That gap is the crack.

The frequency of every letter

Approximate share of each letter in ordinary English text. Exact figures drift a little with the source, but the ranking is remarkably stable.1

The mnemonic The twelve most common letters, in order, spell ETAOIN SHRDLU. The top three — E, T, A — alone make up almost a third of all text. The rarest are J, X, Q, Z.1

How to crack a substitution cipher by hand

Frequency analysis is a guess-and-confirm loop, not a formula. Anchor a few high-value letters, then let the readable fragments pull the rest into place.3

  1. Count the letters. The most frequent cipher letters are most likely E, T, A, O, I, N — start your guesses there.
  2. Use the spaces (if any). A lone letter is A or I; the commonest 3-letter word is THE, which cracks three letters at once.
  3. Hunt doubles. A repeated pair is probably LL, EE, SS, OO, or TT.
  4. Lean on digraphs. After H→? and E→?, look for TH and HE to confirm.
  5. Read, don't solve. Fill partial words and let English finish them — T·E → THE. Wrong guesses produce gibberish; back up and try again.
Why it works — and when it fails Frequency analysis beats any monoalphabetic cipher because the same plaintext letter always maps to the same ciphertext letter, so the language's statistics shine straight through. It weakens on very short messages (too little text to count) and was deliberately defeated by the Vigenère cipher, which uses several alphabets at once — the next lesson.
